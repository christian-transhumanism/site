# CTA Voice & Worldview — drafting reference

Self-contained distillation of how CTA's public pages should sound and what they
draw on. Source of truth for the prose; you should not need to re-read the whole
`cta-wiki` corpus to write in voice. (Upstream sources, for deeper grounding:
`guidelines.txt` at the repo root and `src/cta-wiki/CTA Communications Styleguide.md`.)

These pages are **unsigned**. They speak in CTA's institutional voice — "CTA
approaches…", "We…" — never a named author, never first-person-singular opinion.

---

## 1. What CTA is (use these definitions)

- **Transhumanism** is the ethical use of science and technology to transform the
  human condition.
- **Christian Transhumanism** is the ethical use of science and technology to
  transform the human condition — where *ethical* is defined as discipleship of
  Christ. Alternate formulations (use freely, vary by context):
  - "the use of science and technology to participate in the work of God"
  - "the use of science and technology to participate in the work of Christ and of God"
- **The gospel** is the good news about Christ — the good news about the kingdom of
  the heavens: the cosmic, expanding civilization that transforms dead matter into
  expanding, lasting life. The body of Christ is another name for the same thing.

## 3. The Three Conversions (the argument arc most pages move along)

1. **Ethical Techno-Optimism** — technology can do significant good when handled
   with appropriate ethics; pursue both the technology and the ethics to attain it.
2. **Religious Aspirations through Science** — shared religious visions (theosis,
   immortality, resurrection) can be pursued through science, technology, and ethics.
   *(2.5 — Religious Concepts as Civilizational Infrastructure: religious frameworks
   provide coordination, ethical convergence, and community that secular alternatives
   have failed to replicate; we need them to build the future.)*
3. **Rationalist Religious Cosmology** — the largest structures of reality may
   reflect both our religious visions and our scientific aspirations (e.g.
   simulationism). For the religious: recast religious visions in scientific terms.
   For the secular: let science open the door to religious visions.

## 4. The Three Audiences (decide who a page is for, and pitch to them)

A page should know which audience it's leading with; the framing shifts.

1. **Continuing Christians** — committed believers asking *"is my theology missing
   something?"* Offer a **new vision of what faith can do**. Lead with biblical
   grounding, mission, the bigness of the Christian story.
2. **Deconstructing Christians** — on the edge of faith, asking *"is faith actually
   meaningful, coherent, relevant?"* Offer a **new vision of what faith is**.
   - **2.5 Secular-Rationalist-adjacent deconstructors** need a Christianity that
     speaks to their inner rationalist and earns intellectual credibility.
3. **Secular Rationalists** — outside faith, framed by a scientific worldview, asking
   *"is religion rational?"* Offer a **new way to believe**. Lead with the practical,
   the rational, the civilizational; let faith arrive as a reveal, not a premise.

## 5. Terminology guardrails (do / don't)

These are deliberate. Follow them.

- **Creation Mandate** — not "Dominion Mandate" or "Cultural Mandate." Express as
  "creating and cultivating life," "uplifting life," "taking responsibility for life,"
  "participating in the work of God." A call to be **compassionate creators in the
  image of Christ**, historically understood as a calling to science and technology.
- **Superhuman / Superhumanity** — not "posthuman / posthumanity" for humanity's
  future state.
- **Space Exploration** — not "colonization" or "expansion."
- **Likeness of Christ / Likeness of God** — preferred over a bare "Image of God"
  (which is widely accepted but easily glossed). Pair Image-of-God language with a
  plainer expression: "transformed into the likeness of Christ," "becoming like God."
  *Imago Dei* is for academic contexts; *Theosis* only where the audience knows it
  (Orthodox/Catholic/educated theological). Likeness always includes **both ethical
  and physical** transformation — mention them together.
- **Participating in the work of God / of Christ** — the preferred phrasing for
  participatory theology. We participate; we don't replace.
- **Renewal of All Things** (Matt 19:28; Acts 3:21; Rom 8:18-21; the Lord's Prayer
  "on earth as it is in heaven") — preferred for renewal eschatology.
- **"Working towards"** is usually more accurate than "building" or "creating" when
  talking about the future — though the stronger verbs are right in some places.
- **Faith / works**: we are not saved by works; we are saved by faith *in order that*
  we may do the good works God prepared for us (Eph 2:8-10). Keep this distinction.
- **"Apologetics"** (when it appears): the imperfect fit is **deliberate** — CTA
  occupies the apologetics genre to offer a *new kind* that diminishes the old kind.
  Don't soften it to "Recognition / Witness / Invitation" in outward copy. Extend the
  same respect to "Christian Transhumanism" itself — a term inside a tradition it is
  also reforming.

## 5.5 The Rational Faith move (key reusable framing)

For anything touching reason, faith, rationality, or apologetics, lead with this — it
is one of CTA's sharpest and most characteristic claims, and it is easy to under-shoot:

**Faith is not the opposite of reason; under critical rationalism it is an aspect of
the rational process itself.** Reason (conjecture and critique, Popper → Deutsch)
never delivers certainty — every explanation is provisional. So faith, meaning
*holding to something without complete certainty*, is woven into reasoning, in two
ways:

1. **We act on our best explanations** even knowing we act under uncertainty. To
   commit to your best current understanding while knowing it could be wrong is
   already faith — everyone lives this way, the scientist most of all.
2. **We act on robust-but-not-yet-explained knowledge** — patterns that keep proving
   themselves before we can fully say why. The standing example: the demonstrated
   power of the Christian tradition to grow and advance knowledge — the modern
   growth of scientific knowledge began within a Christian civilization and has
   continued for ~four centuries, unmatched by any other culture. Betting on that
   robust pattern is rational faith, not a flight from evidence.

Do **not** settle for the weaker "faith can at least be reasoned about" — that misses
the point. The claim is stronger: rational inquiry *runs on* faith of this kind.
(State the four-century observation as an observation; don't overclaim mechanism.)

## 6. Voice characteristics (how it should read)

- **Disciplined hope.** Real optimism that takes risk, unequal access, and
  concentrated power seriously. Never naive boosterism; never doom.
- **Informative, not preachy.** These pages teach and frame. A reader who disagrees
  theologically should still find them clear, fair, and worth reading.
- **Practical frameworks land well.** The prototype's hero offers a four-part lens
  (Purpose / Power / Dignity / Wisdom). Concrete questions and named principles beat
  abstract sermonizing.
- **Plain first, rich underneath.** Open in language anyone can follow; let the deeper
  theology and the worldview vocabulary reward the reader who continues. Don't
  jargon-dump up top.
- **Christ as the reveal, not the gate.** Especially for secular-leaning pages, let
  the argument flip toward Christ rather than presupposing him — "the shape of
  progress is the face of Christ" (the Road to Emmaus move).
- **No byline, no hedging-by-attribution.** CTA states its position plainly and names
  its disagreements and open questions honestly (the prototype's "Position,
  disagreements, and open questions").
